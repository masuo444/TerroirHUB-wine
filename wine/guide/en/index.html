INTRODUCTION

What Is Japanese Wine?

Japan's wine industry is one of the world's great hidden stories — 150 years of winemaking, two unique indigenous grapes, and a quality revolution that is now capturing international attention.

Japanese wine (日本ワイン) is wine produced in Japan using 100% domestically grown grapes. This distinction matters: following a landmark 2018 labeling reform, only wines vinified entirely from Japanese fruit may carry the "Japanese wine" designation. What this means in practice is that the 473 wineries in Terroir HUB WINE's database represent producers committed to Japanese terroir — to the particular character of soil, climate, and grape that can only come from Japan's diverse wine regions.

The country produces wine in a surprising range of climates — from Hokkaido in the subarctic north, where cold winters and short summers create wines of striking delicacy and precision, to Yamanashi at the foot of Mt. Fuji, where Japan's most ancient wine grape, Koshu, has been cultivated for over a thousand years. Nagano's high-altitude plateaus produce structured reds that age beautifully. Yamagata's continental climate yields fruit-forward wines of intensity. Each region brings something different to the table.

The 2018 Reform Changed Everything. Before October 2018, wines made from imported grape concentrate could legally be labeled as Japanese wine. The new standards ended this practice. Today, "Japanese wine" on a label is a guarantee that every grape was grown in Japan and vinified domestically — a true geographical promise equivalent to "Cru Beaujolais" or "Napa Valley" in international wine law.
🍇
5 GI Regions
Five Geographical Indication (GI) regions — Yamanashi, Hokkaido, Nagano, Yamagata, and Osaka — operate under strict quality rules comparable to France's AOC system. GI wines must use a minimum percentage of local grapes and meet defined production standards.
🌿
2 Indigenous Grapes
Koshu (white) and Muscat Bailey A (red) are Japan's two internationally recognized indigenous wine grapes. Both are OIV-registered varieties and define Japan's unique contribution to the world of wine. No other country grows them at commercial scale.
🍾
Food Synergy
Japanese wine is built for food. Koshu's mineral delicacy and subtle umami are unmatched partners for sushi and seafood. Muscat Bailey A's soft tannins and fresh fruit slide effortlessly alongside yakitori and teriyaki. The entire wine culture is food-first.

Japan's 5 GI Wine Regions at a Glance

Japan's National Tax Agency (NTA) has designated five Geographical Indication (GI) wine regions. Each operates under strict quality rules requiring a minimum proportion of local grapes and production within the designated zone.

GI Region Designation Year Key Varieties Style Profile
GI Yamanashi 2013 (Japan's first) Koshu, Muscat Bailey A Mineral whites, delicate reds
GI Hokkaido 2018 Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, Kerner Cool-climate elegance
GI Nagano 2021 Merlot, Chardonnay, Pinot Noir Structured, age-worthy reds
GI Yamagata 2021 Dewa33, Muscat Bailey A, Merlot Fruit-forward intensity
GI Osaka 2021 Muscat Bailey A, Delaware Urban winery, light & fresh
