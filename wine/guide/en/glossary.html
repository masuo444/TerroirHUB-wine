LABEL GUIDE

How to Read a Japanese Wine Label

Japanese wine labels follow a distinctive structure. Here is what each element means — for both front and back labels.

日本ワイン
(Nihon Wine)
Since October 2018, this designation on a front label guarantees that the wine is made from 100% Japan-grown grapes and vinified in Japan. The key quality assurance on any Japanese wine label.
GI [地域]
(GI Region)
Indicates Geographical Indication certification. "GI山梨" means GI Yamanashi; "GI北海道" means GI Hokkaido, etc. GI wines use at least 85% locally grown grapes and meet specific production standards.
品種 (Hinshu)
Grape variety (literally "variety/breed"). Will list the grape name — 甲州 (Koshu), マスカット・ベーリーA (Muscat Bailey A), シャルドネ (Chardonnay), メルロー (Merlot), ピノ・ノワール (Pinot Noir), etc.
収穫年/ヴィンテージ
(Vintage)
Harvest year. Some labels show the year in Western format (2023); older bottles may use the Japanese era system (令和5年 = 2023, Reiwa 5th year). Heisei 元年 = 1989; Reiwa 元年 = 2019.
辛口 / 甘口
(Dry / Sweet)
辛口 (karakuchi) means dry. 甘口 (amakuchi) means sweet. 中辛口 (naka-karakuchi) means off-dry / medium-dry. Most premium Japanese wines are 辛口.
アルコール度数
(Alcohol %)
Alcohol content by volume. Koshu wines are often 11–12.5% ABV. Nagano reds are typically 12.5–13.5%. Note that Japanese wine in general tends toward lower alcohol than equivalent European styles — intentionally so, for food pairing.
シュール・リー
(Sur Lie)
If this phrase appears on a Koshu label, the wine has been aged on its lees (spent yeast cells) — giving additional texture, body, and a subtle brioche note. A quality indicator for Koshu.
酸化防止剤無添加
(No Sulfites Added)
Indicates no sulfur dioxide was added as a preservative. Common on natural wine labels. Note: "no sulfites added" does not mean the wine contains zero sulfites — fermentation naturally produces small amounts.
醸造元 (Jōzōmoto)
Winery / producer (literally "brewing origin"). The company that made the wine. Important to distinguish from 販売元 (hanbaemoto) — the distributor or retailer, which may be different from the producer.
内容量 (Naiyōryō)
Volume (contents amount). Standard bottles are 750ml. Half-bottles (375ml) and magnums (1.5L) are also produced by some wineries.

A

Acidity (酸味, Sanmi)
さんみ — Sanmi
The tart, mouth-watering quality in wine derived from natural grape acids (primarily tartaric and malic acid). Japanese wines, grown in cooler climates or harvested before full ripeness, often retain naturally high acidity — a key reason they pair so well with food. Good acidity is described as "lively" or "fresh"; too much is "sharp" or "harsh."
Amakuchi (甘口)
あまくち — Ama-ku-chi
Sweet. Wine labeled 甘口 has perceptible residual sugar — typically from 20g/L upward. Delaware and some Muscat Bailey A wines are made in a slightly amakuchi style. Contrast with 辛口 (karakuchi, dry).
Anthocyanin (アントシアニン)
Natural pigment compounds responsible for red wine's color. Muscat Bailey A and Dewa33 have moderate anthocyanin levels; deeper-colored Japanese reds from Cabernet Sauvignon or Merlot have higher concentrations.
AOC / Appellation (産地呼称)
France's AOC (Appellation d'Origine Contrôlée) system is the European equivalent of Japan's GI system. Both systems protect geographic wine names and enforce quality standards based on origin, permitted grape varieties, and production methods.
Assemblage (アッサンブラージュ)
French term for blending different lots, varieties, or parcels of wine to create the final cuvée. Common practice in Yamanashi, where many small-lot Koshu wines are blended from multiple farmers' grapes.

B

Brix (糖度, Tōdo)
とうど — Tō-do
A measure of the sugar content of grape juice, used to determine harvest timing. One degree Brix approximates 1% potential alcohol. Koshu is typically harvested at 18–20° Brix for dry wine styles. Nagano Merlot aims for 22–24° Brix to achieve full phenolic maturity. Brix is one of the key variables winemakers track during the ripening season.
Biodynamic (ビオディナミ)
ビオディナミ — Bio-di-na-mi
A holistic farming philosophy (developed by Rudolf Steiner) treating the vineyard as a self-sustaining ecosystem, following a lunar planting calendar and using specific plant and mineral preparations. Rare but growing in Japan; Coco Farm & Winery (Tochigi) is a notable practitioner.

C

Campbell Early (キャンベル・アーリー)
North American hybrid red grape widely grown in Japan for table grapes and wine. Common in Niigata, Yamagata, and Kyushu. Produces light, grapey reds for casual consumption; rarely serious wine.
Château (シャトー)
French term for an estate winery, often with its own vineyards. Used in Japan by several major producers (Château Mercian, Château Sakaori) as a quality signal and marketing designation. Not a legally regulated term in Japan.
Concrete Egg (コンクリート・エッグ)
Ovoid concrete fermentation vessel. The egg shape creates natural convection currents during fermentation, producing wines of unusual textural complexity without oak influence. Growing trend among premium Japanese producers.

D

Delaware (デラウェア)
でらうぇあ
A North American hybrid white grape (Vitis labrusca origin) widely grown in Niigata and Yamanashi. Produces aromatic, slightly sweet wines with lychee and muscat character. Originally a table grape; now also an important wine grape, especially for Niigata sparkling wines.
Dewa33 (出羽33)
でわ33 — De-wa 33
A red wine grape hybrid developed in Yamagata Prefecture as a cross between Muscat Bailey A and Campbell Early. The "33" refers to Showa year 33 (1958) when development was completed. Produces vivid, fruit-forward reds uniquely suited to Yamagata's continental climate. The defining variety of GI Yamagata.
Domaine (ドメーヌ)
French term for a wine estate where the producer grows their own grapes and vinifies them. Domaine Takahiko (Hokkaido) is Japan's most famous example. Contrasts with négociant producers who buy grapes from other growers.

G

GI (Geographical Indication / 地理的表示)
ちりてきひょうじ
Japan's NTA-administered system protecting wine names based on geographic origin. GI wines must use a minimum percentage of locally grown grapes (typically 85%+) and be vinified within the zone. Japan has five wine GIs: Yamanashi (2013), Hokkaido (2018), Nagano (2021), Yamagata (2021), and Osaka (2021). Equivalent to France's AOC or Italy's DOC.
Gyōsha (業者)
ぎょうしゃ
Trade/industry operator. In wine context, refers to the commercial producer or bottler. On Japanese labels, 製造者 (seizōsha) is "manufacturer/producer"; 販売者 (hanbaisha) is "seller/distributor." These may differ when wine is produced at a négociant facility.

K

Karakuchi (辛口)
からくち — Ka-ra-ku-chi
Dry. The opposite of amakuchi (sweet). Premium Japanese wines are virtually always 辛口. The term literally means "spicy/sharp mouth" — referring to the absence of sweetness rather than heat.
Kerner (ケルナー)
German white grape (Trollinger × Riesling cross) that is one of Hokkaido's most important varieties. Produces Riesling-like wines with fresh acidity, white peach, and floral aromatics. GI Hokkaido-certified Kerner is among Japan's most distinctive white wines.
Koshu (甲州)
こうしゅう — Kō-shū
Japan's most important indigenous white wine grape, cultivated in Yamanashi Prefecture for over 1,000 years. Produces mineral, delicate white wines with citrus peel, white peach, and subtle umami. Thin-skinned and pinkish-grey in color. OIV-registered since 2010. The grape most closely associated with Japanese wine's identity internationally.
Koji (麹)
こうじ
The mold Aspergillus oryzae, central to Japanese fermentation culture (sake, miso, soy sauce). Not directly used in winemaking, but relevant because Koshu wine's amino acid profile resonates with koji-fermented foods — one reason Koshu pairs so well with Japanese cuisine.

M

Malolactic Fermentation (MLF / マロラクティック発酵)
Conversion of sharp malic acid to softer lactic acid by bacteria. Standard in red wine production; less common in Koshu, which benefits from its natural malic acidity for freshness. Sur Lie Koshu may undergo partial MLF to soften texture.
Muscat Bailey A (マスカット・ベーリーA)
ますかっと・べーりーA — MBA
Japan's most widely planted red wine grape, developed in 1927 by Zenbei Kawakami at Iwanohara Vineyard, Niigata. A cross of Bailey and Muscat Hamburg. Produces light ruby wines with strawberry candy aromas (foxy character), low tannin, and fresh acidity. Food-friendly and widely available. OIV-registered 2013.
Mizunara (水楢)
みずなら — Japanese white oak
Japanese white oak (Quercus mongolica). Famously used for Japanese whisky barrel aging, imparting sandalwood, coconut, and spice notes. Occasionally used for wine barrel aging by a small number of Japanese producers — a costly curiosity that adds a distinctly Japanese dimension to the wine.

N

Nagano (長野)
ながの
Japan's leading high-altitude wine region. GI Nagano (designated 2021) covers vineyards at 600–900m elevation with up to 15°C day-night temperature variation. Famous for Merlot (Kikyogahara district), Chardonnay, and Pinot Noir. Home to Château Mercian Mariko Winery and Kikyogahara Winery.
Natural Wine (ナチュラルワイン)
No official legal definition, but generally means: organic or biodynamic viticulture, wild yeast fermentation, minimal sulfur addition, no or minimal fining and filtration. Japan's natural wine scene is vibrant, centered in Yamanashi and Hokkaido.
Nihon Wine / Japanese Wine (日本ワイン)
にほんわいん
Since October 2018, the legally protected term for wine made in Japan using 100% Japan-grown grapes. The labeling reform eliminated ambiguity about "domestic wine" that may have been made with imported grape concentrate. The designation is Japan's most important quality guarantee for wine consumers.

O

OIV (国際ぶどう・ワイン機構)
International Organisation of Vine and Wine. The global intergovernmental organization that maintains the register of internationally recognized grape varieties. Koshu was registered in 2010; Muscat Bailey A in 2013 — making these the only two Japanese varieties with OIV recognition.
Orange Wine (オレンジワイン)
White wine made with extended skin contact during fermentation, giving the wine an amber/copper color, additional phenolic structure, and savory, textural character. Also called "skin-contact wine." Applied to Koshu in Japan, where producers leave the pinkish skins in contact with the juice for days or weeks to extract phenols and anthocyanins. The result is a uniquely Japanese interpretation of orange wine.

P

Pétillant Naturel (ペティアン・ナチュレル / Pet-nat)
A lightly sparkling wine produced by bottling before primary fermentation is complete, trapping natural CO2 in the bottle. Typically cloudy (unfiltered), with a fresh, yeasty character. Sealed with a crown cap. Growing niche in Japan, particularly for Koshu pet-nat from Yamanashi natural wine producers. Consumed young; not designed for aging.

R

Residual Sugar (残糖, Zantō)
ざんとう
The amount of sugar remaining in wine after fermentation. Dry wines have under 4g/L residual sugar; off-dry wines 4–12g/L; sweet wines above 45g/L. Koshu dry wines are typically under 3g/L; Delaware wines are often 15–35g/L.

S

Sur Lie (シュール・リー)
French term meaning "on the lees." Describes aging wine in contact with the spent yeast cells from fermentation. The yeast cells decompose (autolysis), releasing complex flavor compounds: creaminess, bread, yeast, and a slight reduction character. Standard technique for premium Koshu production; aging may last 3–18 months. The technique was adapted from Muscadet production in France's Loire Valley.
Sparkling Wine (スパークリングワイン)
Japan produces sparkling wine by several methods: méthode traditionnelle (Champagne method, secondary fermentation in bottle), Charmat method (secondary fermentation in pressurized tank), and pétillant naturel (see above). Sparkling Koshu is a growing category; Grace Wine and Lumière both produce high-quality méthode traditionnelle examples.
Sulfites / Sulfur Dioxide (亜硫酸塩)
ありゅうさんえん
SO2 used as an antioxidant and antimicrobial agent in winemaking. Naturally present at low levels in all wine. Added sulfites preserve freshness and prevent oxidation. Japanese regulations require "亜硫酸塩使用" (sulfites used) on the label if added. Natural wine producers minimize or eliminate sulfite additions.

T

Tanashitate (棚仕立て)
たなしたて
The traditional Japanese pergola trellis system where grapevines are trained horizontally overhead on a flat canopy structure. Workers walk beneath the trellis to harvest. The system maximizes airflow, reducing humidity and disease pressure in Japan's wet summers — particularly important for thin-skinned Koshu. Still the dominant viticulture system in Yamanashi, though vertical VSP (vertical shoot positioning) is gaining ground at quality-focused producers.
Tannin (タンニン)
Phenolic compounds in grape skins, seeds, and stems that create astringency and structure in red wines. Japanese red wines generally feature lower tannin than European equivalents — partly a function of grape varieties (MBA has naturally soft tannin) and partly a result of gentle extraction techniques. Nagano Merlot and Cabernet Sauvignon are Japan's most tannic wines.
Terroir (テロワール)
てろわーる
The French concept describing how a wine's character is shaped by its complete growing environment — soil, climate, topography, and even human viticultural tradition. Japan's terroir is uniquely complex: volcanic soils, dramatic elevation changes, monsoon climate, and centuries of indigenous variety cultivation combine to produce wines unlike anything made elsewhere. Terroir HUB WINE exists to document and celebrate Japan's wine terroir.

V

Vintage (ヴィンテージ / 収穫年)
しゅうかくねん — Shūkaku-nen
The year the grapes were harvested. Vintage variation is significant in Japan — particularly Hokkaido and Nagano, where weather can change dramatically year to year. 2019, 2021, and 2022 are widely considered excellent vintages for Yamanashi Koshu; 2018 and 2020 brought difficult conditions. Always check vintage year when buying premium Japanese wine for aging.
VSP (Vertical Shoot Positioning / 垂直栽培)
Modern trellis system where shoots are trained vertically upward, exposing grapes to sunlight while maintaining the trunk vertically. More efficient than tanashitate for fruit quality management and increasingly adopted by quality-focused Japanese producers, particularly in Nagano and Hokkaido.

Y

Yamanashi (山梨)
やまなし
Japan's most historically significant wine prefecture, home to GI Yamanashi (Japan's first wine GI, designated 2013). Located at the foot of Mt. Fuji in a mountain basin, Yamanashi has over 90 wineries and is the heartland of Koshu cultivation. The Katsunuma area has the highest density of wineries in Japan.
Yoichi (余市)
よいち
A coastal town in western Hokkaido, Japan's most celebrated sub-region for Pinot Noir. Yoichi's cool oceanic climate and granite-clay soils produce Pinot Noir of remarkable precision and complexity. Domaine Takahiko's "Nou" Pinot Noir from Yoichi has become one of Japan's most collectible wines.
Yuzu (柚子)
ゆず
A Japanese citrus fruit (similar to Meyer lemon crossed with mandarin) with an intensely aromatic rind. Not a wine ingredient, but frequently mentioned in Koshu tasting notes because Koshu's citrus character has an unmistakable yuzu-like quality — fragrant, slightly bitter peel, and a clean, bright finish.
Zweigelt (ツヴァイゲルト)
Austrian red grape (Blaufränkisch × St. Laurent) that thrives in Hokkaido's cool climate. Produces wines of lively acidity, crunchy red fruit, and a characteristic peppery note. An underappreciated variety that rewards Hokkaido wine explorers.
